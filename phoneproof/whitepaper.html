PhoneProof

Decentralized Mobile Identity Verification Protocol

Version 1.0 | December 2024

Abstract

PhoneProof introduces a revolutionary blockchain-based identity verification system that leverages mobile device ownership as proof of unique human identity. By combining zero-knowledge proofs, decentralized verification networks, and biometric authentication, PhoneProof creates a privacy-preserving, sybil-resistant identity layer for Web3 applications without requiring centralized KYC processes or compromising user anonymity.

1. Introduction

1.1 The Identity Crisis in Web3

Current blockchain ecosystems face a critical challenge: verifying unique human identity without sacrificing decentralization or privacy. Traditional solutions rely on either centralized KYC providers or easily exploitable proof-of-humanity mechanisms. PhoneProof solves this through cryptographic verification of mobile device ownership, leveraging the fact that phone numbers remain one of the most reliable indicators of unique human identity in the digital age.

1.2 Why Mobile-First Verification?

  • Over 6.8 billion unique mobile subscribers globally (2024)
  • Strict telecom regulations prevent mass phone number acquisition
  • SIM cards tied to government-issued IDs in most jurisdictions
  • Built-in secure elements for cryptographic operations
  • Universal accessibility across demographics and geographies

2. Technical Architecture

2.1 Core Components

Zero-Knowledge Phone Verification

Users prove phone number ownership without revealing the actual number. Utilizes zk-SNARKs to generate proofs of SMS verification codes received from a decentralized oracle network.

Decentralized Verification Network (DVN)

Network of independent telecom gateways that send verification codes and attest to delivery without storing phone numbers. Validators stake tokens and earn fees for honest verification.

Biometric Anchoring (Optional)

Device-local biometric data (fingerprint/face) creates additional proof layer without biometric data ever leaving the device. Utilizes Trusted Execution Environments (TEE) on modern smartphones.

Identity NFT

Soul-bound NFT representing verified unique identity. Non-transferable, revocable upon proof of compromise, and compatible with existing Web3 infrastructure.

2.2 Verification Flow

  1. Registration: User initiates verification through PhoneProof-enabled dApp
  2. Hashing: Phone number hashed with salt on client-side
  3. Oracle Selection: Random DVN validator selected via VRF
  4. SMS Dispatch: Validator sends code via anonymized telecom gateway
  5. Proof Generation: User generates zk-SNARK proving code receipt
  6. On-Chain Verification: Smart contract validates proof and mints Identity NFT
  7. Reputation Building: NFT accumulates on-chain reputation over time

3. Privacy & Security

3.1 Privacy Guarantees

  • Phone Number Privacy: Never stored on-chain or by validators
  • Linkability Resistance: Hash salts prevent cross-platform tracking
  • Selective Disclosure: Users control what identity attributes to reveal
  • Right to Erasure: Users can revoke and regenerate identities

3.2 Attack Resistance

Sybil Attacks: Economic + regulatory barriers to phone number acquisition make mass identity farming prohibitively expensive.

Validator Collusion: Multi-validator consensus with slashing for dishonest behavior. Minimum 3/5 validator agreement required.

SIM Swapping: Multi-factor verification including device fingerprinting and optional biometrics. Cooling period for identity transfers.

Privacy Leakage: Zero-knowledge architecture ensures even compromised validators cannot deanonymize users.
